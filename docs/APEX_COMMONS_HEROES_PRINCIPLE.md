## Overview

This document contains the finalized, production-ready content for the "Heroes" principle at Apex Commons. This content addresses the human tendency toward hero worship while maintaining nuance and gratitude for those who do good work. Uses approved archetypal language (no specific celebrity names).

## Content Block

### Title
**A Note on Heroes**

### Subtitle
**Admire the work. Don't worship the person.**

### Location
Place this in the **"Community Principles"** or **"About"** section of apexcommons.org

### Full Content

> At Apex Commons, we are honest about a simple human truth: we love heroes. We look for avatars of our values—singers, founders, activists—and we put them on pedestals.
>
> That instinct isn't stupid; it comes from a desire to see good prevail. But we refuse to turn people into gods.
>
> Even the people who do the most visible good—the humanitarians, the innovators, the truth-tellers—are still human. They have been both harmed and harmful. They make tradeoffs we don't see.
>
> **Our stance is gratitude, not idolatry.**
> * We celebrate people who use their power well.
> * We stay clear-eyed about their limits.
> * We remember the invisible systems and workers behind every famous face.
>
> No leader is your conscience. We can learn from them, be moved by them, and still keep our own moral agency. We build cultures where no one has to be a god for good things to happen.

## Content Philosophy

### Core Messages
1. **Honest acknowledgment** - We all have hero-worship tendencies
2. **Validation without indulgence** - The instinct comes from a good place
3. **Complexity over simplicity** - Heroes are humans with flaws
4. **Agency over dependency** - Keep your own moral compass
5. **Systems over individuals** - Recognize collective effort

### What This Content Does
- ✅ Names a common human behavior without shame
- ✅ Sets community norms for how we discuss public figures
- ✅ Protects against cult-of-personality dynamics
- ✅ Encourages critical thinking and nuance
- ✅ Values contribution without deification

### What This Content Avoids
- ❌ Cynicism or dismissal of genuinely good work
- ❌ "Call-out culture" toward past heroes
- ❌ Nihilism ("no one is good")
- ❌ Preachy or condescending tone
- ❌ Specific examples that could date poorly

## Community Moderation Guidelines

### How Moderators Should Apply This

**Encourage:**
- ✅ Specific appreciation ("Their work on X was brilliant")
- ✅ Nuanced takes ("They did Y well, but Z has issues")
- ✅ Crediting teams and systems
- ✅ Learning from work without adopting all views

**Gently Redirect:**
- ⚠️ Uncritical praise ("They can do no wrong")
- ⚠️ Assuming perfection ("They'd never do something bad")
- ⚠️ Seeking moral guidance from celebrities
- ⚠️ Ignoring contributions of others

**Firmly Address:**
- 🚫 Cult-like behavior
- 🚫 Harassment of those who criticize heroes
- 🚫 Conspiracy theories about hero's critics
- 🚫 Dismissing valid concerns as "cancellation"

## Appendix: The Archetypal Language Choice

### Why No Specific Names?

We deliberately avoid naming specific celebrities, activists, or founders in this principle because:

1. **Timelessness** - Specific people fall in/out of favor; principles endure
2. **Universality** - Applies to all public figures, not just current ones
3. **Avoids irony** - Don't create new heroes while warning against hero worship
4. **Focus on behavior** - The pattern matters more than the person
5. **Safety** - Protects us from being tied to anyone's future actions

### Approved Language
- ✅ "singers, founders, activists" (archetypes)
- ✅ "humanitarians, innovators, truth-tellers" (roles)
- ✅ "leaders" (general)
- ✅ "people who use their power well" (behavior-focused)

### Avoid
- ❌ Specific celebrity names
- ❌ Current events references
- ❌ Named controversies
- ❌ Dated cultural references

This keeps the principle **eternal** and **universally applicable**.
