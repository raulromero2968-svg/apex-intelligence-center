## Introduction

The Pokemon TCG market is experiencing activity levels rivaling the 2021 boom, driven by Pokemon TCG Pocket's digital success and strong sales from Surging Sparks and Prismatic Evolutions<sup>1</sup>. Modern Illustration Rares that traded at a few dollars in September 2024 doubled within three months, then doubled again within days<sup>2</sup>.

One Piece ranked fifth among best-selling TCGs in 2024, with the Wings of the Captain booster box leading sales<sup>3</sup>. PSA submissions for 2024 Pokemon sets show significant population growth: Temporal Forces reached 116,553 graded cards, Paldean Fates hit 158,733, and Surging Sparks accumulated 238,737 submissions<sup>4</sup>.

## Method & Data

This analysis aggregates data from TCGPlayer's monthly price trend reports, PSA population databases accessed January 2025, and market sales data from major platforms<sup>5</sup>. Price data represents median sold listings from October 2024 through January 2025. Limitations include potential sampling bias toward higher-value cards and regional price variations between US and international markets.

Grading turnaround times averaged 18-24 business days for economy service tiers across PSA, BGS, and CGC during Q4 2024 and Q1 2025<sup>6</sup>. Population data reflects submissions processed through January 10, 2025.

## Findings

Alt-Art Latias & Latios-GX exceeded $2,000 market price, eclipsing Moonbreon which never surpassed $1,750<sup>7</sup>. However, Moonbreon crossed the $2,000 threshold for the first time following a September 10 buyout event<sup>8</sup>. Pre-pandemic cards from sets printed before production exploded during 2020-2021 drove significant price appreciation as collectors sought lower-population items<sup>9</sup>.

Japanese Pokemon cards command premiums over English equivalents. A common Drifloon from Scarlet & Violet base set sells for $0.04 in English versus $2.43 for the Japanese version, while a Pikachu from Celebrations trades at $0.45 in English versus $20.13 in Japanese<sup>10</sup>. These differences reflect smaller Japanese print runs serving a geographically limited market versus English versions distributed across multiple countries<sup>11</sup>.

PSA 10 population rates for 2022-2024 Pokemon sets average 48% compared to 18% for pre-2016 sets, creating grade premium compression where modern PSA 10s trade near raw card values plus grading costs<sup>12</sup>. Analysis indicates 18% of all Pokemon cards ever produced were printed in 2023-2024<sup>13</sup>.

## Implications

Modern card investors face population inflation risk from elevated PSA 10 rates. Cards with PSA 10 populations exceeding 1,000 examples show annual appreciation of 4-7% versus 12-18% for populations under 50<sup>14</sup>. Vintage sealed products and pre-2010 graded cards offer defensive positioning during market volatility<sup>15</sup>.

Japanese exclusive releases may provide growth exposure given structural scarcity from limited print runs, though liquidity remains concentrated in English-language markets representing 71% of transaction volume<sup>16</sup>. Collectors should account for reprint risk in sets under five years old, particularly as Pokemon company has increased modern set production capacity<sup>17</sup>.
