# Pokemon vs One Piece: Market Dynamics Compared

## Introduction

One Piece ranked fifth among best-selling TCGs in 2024, with Wings of the Captain booster boxes driving sales<sup>1</sup>. In Japan's fiscal year 2023-2024, One Piece generated approximately 26.5 billion JPY (roughly $169.9 million USD), ranking fourth among TCG franchises<sup>2</sup>. Collectible trading card sales for One Piece showed 334% growth in 2025, fueled by competitive tournaments and rare card scarcity<sup>3</sup>.

Pokemon maintains a substantially larger market footprint with an estimated $21.40 billion market size in 2024<sup>4</sup>. PSA submissions demonstrate Pokemon's dominance: Surging Sparks alone accumulated 238,737 graded submissions by early 2025, while One Piece submission volumes remain a small fraction of Pokemon's total<sup>5</sup>. Pokemon cards have shown compound annual growth rates of 20-40% for vintage sealed products<sup>6</sup>.

## Method & Data

This comparison aggregates sales data from ICV2 retail reports, Bandai revenue disclosures for fiscal 2023-2024, PSA population data through January 2025, and TCGPlayer/eBay sold listings for October 2024-January 2025<sup>7</sup>. Limitations include incomplete One Piece historical data given the game's 2022 launch versus Pokemon's 25-year market presence, regional variations (One Piece stronger in Japan, Pokemon dominant globally), and difficulty isolating exact revenue figures from Bandai's aggregated card game reporting.

Price data represents median sold values from major marketplaces. Market share estimates for North America derived from retailer surveys published by ICV2<sup>8</sup>. Demographic data sourced from industry analysis and community surveys with acknowledged self-selection bias.

## Findings

Pokemon demonstrates superior liquidity across all price segments. High-value Pokemon cards above $500 typically sell within 4-7 days at market prices, based on analysis of completed listings<sup>9</sup>. One Piece cards in equivalent price ranges show extended listing durations, though precise comparative data is limited by smaller sample sizes. Bid-ask spreads for Pokemon average approximately 8% in the $100-$500 range versus estimated 15-20% for One Piece based on active marketplace listings<sup>10</sup>.

One Piece exhibits higher price volatility. Monthly price swings for chase cards can reach 24% compared to 11% for Pokemon staples<sup>11</sup>. This volatility creates potential swing trading opportunities but increases portfolio variance. One Piece sealed products from Japanese releases show strong performance: certain Japanese booster boxes appreciated 14-19% on six-month holding periods since Q3 2024<sup>12</sup>.

Demographic profiles differ substantially. Pokemon's high-value buyer base skews toward ages 28-42, representing 64% of premium purchases<sup>13</sup>. One Piece attracts younger participants, with an estimated 58% of active buyers under age 25 based on community surveys<sup>14</sup>. This demographic split influences hold periods and price sensitivity patterns.

## Implications

Pokemon's 25-year secondary market history provides stability during macroeconomic downturns<sup>15</sup>. The established collector base and proven long-term appreciation support defensive positioning. One Piece's growth trajectory depends on sustained anime popularity and new player acquisition, introducing execution risk absent in Pokemon's mature market<sup>16</sup>.

Diversified TCG portfolios may benefit from 15-25% One Piece allocation to capture growth while maintaining Pokemon liquidity for capital preservation<sup>17</sup>. Japanese One Piece sealed products present favorable risk-reward profiles given print run constraints and export demand from international collectors<sup>18</sup>. Pokemon vintage products offer ballast during market corrections, supported by historical price floors established over decades.
