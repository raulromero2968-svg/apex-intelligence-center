📚 Why Tax Optimization Matters for TCG Collectors

Most TCG collectors don't realize they're sitting on tax optimization opportunities. Whether you're buying, selling, trading, or just holding - the IRS wants to know about it. Here's why these tools are essential:

1. Collectibles are Taxed Differently

Trading cards are classified as "collectibles" by the IRS. Unlike stocks (20% max tax), collectibles are taxed at up to 28% for long-term gains. This makes tax planning even more critical for TCG investors.

2. Tax Loss Harvesting is Legal and Powerful

The same strategy Wall Street uses for stocks works for cards. Sell losers, claim the deduction, offset gains. With a $50K collection, it's not uncommon to find $5,000-$10,000 in harvestable losses during market corrections.

3. Timing Matters More Than You Think

Holding a card for 366 days vs 365 days can mean the difference between 37% tax (short-term) and 28% tax (long-term). On a $10,000 gain, that's $900 in savings for waiting one more day.

4. Diversification Reduces Risk

Pokemon collectors learned this the hard way in 2022 when the market corrected 30-40%. Those who were diversified across MTG, Yu-Gi-Oh!, and One Piece lost far less. Our analyzer helps you avoid concentration risk.

5. The IRS Expects You to Report

Selling cards on eBay, TCGPlayer, or in person? The IRS considers this taxable income. Platforms are required to report transactions over $600. Our Portfolio Performance Report helps you stay compliant and organized.
