📚 Understanding IRS Schedule D

Schedule D is the IRS form used to report capital gains and losses from the sale of investment property, including trading cards and collectibles.

What You Need to File:

  • Form 8949: Lists each individual transaction (card sale)
  • Schedule D: Summarizes totals from Form 8949
  • Form 1040: Your main tax return where Schedule D totals transfer

Key Information for Each Transaction:

  • Description of property (card name and details)
  • Date acquired (purchase date)
  • Date sold (sale date)
  • Sales proceeds (sale price)
  • Cost basis (purchase price + fees)
  • Gain or loss (proceeds - cost basis)

Capital Loss Limitations:

If you have a net capital loss:

  • You can deduct up to $3,000 ($1,500 if married filing separately) against ordinary income
  • Losses exceeding $3,000 can be carried forward to future tax years indefinitely
  • Capital losses first offset capital gains, then up to $3,000 of ordinary income

Record Keeping:

The IRS requires you to keep records for at least 3 years after filing. For collectibles, keep:

  • Purchase receipts (eBay invoices, card shop receipts)
  • Sale receipts (PayPal statements, platform transaction history)
  • Photos of cards sold (especially high-value items)
  • Grading invoices (PSA, BGS fees add to cost basis)

Pro Tips:

  • Export this report and give it to your CPA - it has everything they need
  • File even if you have losses - they reduce your tax bill
  • Collectibles are taxed at a maximum 28% rate (vs 20% for stocks) if held long-term
  • Consider using tax software like TurboTax or H&R Block that integrates with Schedule D
