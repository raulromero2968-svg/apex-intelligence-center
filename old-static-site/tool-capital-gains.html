📚 Understanding Capital Gains Tax

When you sell a trading card for more than you paid, the profit is a capital gain and is taxable. The tax rate depends on how long you held the card.

Short-term vs Long-term:

  • Short-term (held ≤ 1 year): Taxed as ordinary income at your tax bracket rate (10%-37%)
  • Long-term (held > 1 year): Taxed at preferential capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20%)

Long-term Capital Gains Rates (2024-2025):

  • 0%: Income up to $44,625 (single) or $89,250 (married)
  • 15%: Income $44,626 - $492,300 (single) or $89,251 - $553,850 (married)
  • 20%: Income over $492,300 (single) or $553,850 (married)

The Power of Patience:

Waiting just one day past the 1-year mark can save you thousands. If you're in the 24% tax bracket and sell a card with a $10,000 gain:

  • Short-term: $2,400 in taxes (24%)
  • Long-term: $1,500 in taxes (15%)
  • Savings: $900 by waiting 366+ days!

State Taxes:

Most states also tax capital gains. Rates vary from 0% (no state income tax states like Florida, Texas, Nevada) to 13.3% (California). Always factor in your state tax rate for accurate calculations.

Pro Tips:

  • Track purchase dates meticulously - they determine short vs long-term treatment
  • If selling at year-end, consider timing to optimize your tax year
  • Large gains? Consider selling over multiple years to avoid bracket creep
  • Offset gains with losses using tax loss harvesting
